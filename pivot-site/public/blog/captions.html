Captions that sound like your kitchen, not a template

Most food brands write captions that read like a disclaimer. "Our acclaimed seasonal brunch specials, now available for limited time." No one talks like that, and no one scrolls past it to stick around.

The three-sentence pattern

  • Name the dish and why it exists. "The morcilla jam is back because it's February and the line deserves a reason to stand in the cold."
  • Add one detail only you know. Burn time, family note, the fact it sells out by eight. Specifics beat superlatives every time.
  • Give a tiny reason to act. Hours, count, or the next batch date. A soft nudge, never a countdown circle.

Before → after:

"Our award-winning chimmi sauce, crafted with premium ingredients."

"The chimmi took three years to get right because we refused to bottle it until it bit back. Tonight, jar #1 of the new batch — come see if we got it."

Keep the streak alive

Batch three captions in ten minutes using the pattern. If you can't give one detail only you know, you're not describing your food — you're describing a category. That's the single highest-leverage fix most brands have.
