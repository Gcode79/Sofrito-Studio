The universal baseline

One duty, three habits

Under the REALTOR® Code of Ethics, Article 2 bars misrepresenting facts about a property, and Article 12 requires marketing to present a true picture. Every board’s staging rule is a local spelling of that same duty. In practice it comes down to three habits:

  1. Label on the image. The staged photo itself carries the words “Virtually Staged,” where a buyer cannot miss them.
  2. Disclosure in the remarks. The listing text says, in plain words, which photos are virtually staged.
  3. Original kept beside it. The unaltered photo of the same room stays with the staged one, so anyone can compare.

Two boards spell the practice out almost word for word. MLS Now’s Rule 3.3 calls for the on-image “Virtually Staged” label with the originals retained; CVR MLS’s Rule 5.8.2 calls for the label, the remarks disclosure, and an unaltered photo of the same view beside the staged one. Where a board’s text differs, the board’s text wins — which is why we confirm it per order.

The line we hand you. Every delivery includes a remarks disclosure ready to paste, in this shape: “Photos 3–7 of this listing are virtually staged. The original, unstaged photos appear alongside them.” Your board’s exact wording, where it has one, is what we follow.

Rule on the books

ARMLS — Arizona

ARMLS treats a staged photo as a digitally altered image, and its Rule 8.23 is written for exactly that case: the alteration must be disclosed, and in Flexmls the original photo sits adjacent to the altered one, so the comparison is one glance away. Enforcement tightens from December 2026, with $200 fines on the table.

What we do on an ARMLS listing: label on the image, disclosure in the remarks, original placed beside the staged photo — the board’s own pattern, built into the delivery.

Source: ARMLS published rules (Rule 8.23), checked October 2026.

State law

California — AB 723

In California this is not just MLS policy; it is state law. Assembly Bill 723, in effect since January 1, 2026, requires digitally altered listing images to be disclosed and the unaltered original to be made available — and an undisclosed altered image is a misdemeanor.

What we do on a California listing: the same three habits, treated as a legal requirement rather than a courtesy — label, disclosure, original beside it.

Source: California Assembly Bill 723 (2025), effective January 1, 2026.

The other half of disclosure

What staging must never do

A label does not excuse a lie. However a board phrases it, the same things are off limits — the rules above assume them, and so do we:

  • Move, remove, or resize a wall, window, door, floor, or fixture.
  • Hide a defect — a stain, a crack, damage of any kind.
  • Invent a feature, or a view, that the home does not have.
  • Add people or animals.
