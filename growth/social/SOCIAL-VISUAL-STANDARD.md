## Core directive

Never default to line art, clip art, flat vector illustration, generic AI illustration,
abstract blobs, generic quote cards, stock office scenes, or "agency-style" graphics.

For every social post, create a realistic, high-attention visual concept designed to
make a food-business owner stop scrolling, understand the problem quickly, and visit
sofritostudio.com when the post is relevant to their needs.

The visual must feel:
- Real
- Cinematic or editorial
- Food-literate
- Specific to food-business ownership
- Mobile-first
- Human
- Credible
- Useful
- Native to social media, not like a brochure or presentation slide

The visual is not decoration. It must communicate the post's core idea in the first
one to two seconds.

---

## Visual hierarchy

Choose visuals in this order of preference:

1. Original approved photography or video
- Founder, team, studio process, real food-business environment
- Real restaurant, café, food truck, kitchen, market, packaging, menu, or service footage
- Permissioned client materials only

2. Realistic photography or video from licensed sources
- Use only sources whose commercial license/permission is confirmed
- Track source, license, creator, and usage status in `growth/social/asset-register.md`

3. Realistic AI-generated imagery
- Use only when original or properly licensed footage is unavailable
- The image must look photographic and credible, not illustrative
- Never use AI imagery to imply a real client, real project, real venue, real outcome,
  real review, real employee, or documented event
- Label internally as "conceptual AI image"
- Do not use a recognizable person, trademark, restaurant identity, or copied brand asset

4. Text-led design only
- Use only when the insight is strong enough to stand alone
- Use real material texture, editorial typography, subtle photography, or a realistic
  environmental background—not line art or decorative illustration

## Required visual thinking before creation

For each post, first define:

- Audience: Exact food-business owner or operator
- Scroll-stop moment: What would make them pause within two seconds?
- Emotional trigger: Curiosity, recognition, relief, ambition, frustration, pride, contrast,
  urgency, or "that is exactly my problem"
- Visual subject: One dominant real-world object, person, action, or scene
- Message hierarchy: What must be understood first, second, and third?
- Desired action: Save, share, visit sofritostudio.com, book a Sofrito Session, or inquire
- Asset type: Original, licensed, permissioned client material, or conceptual AI image
- Permission status: Approved, licensed, needs approval, or unavailable

Do not create visual assets until this brief is completed.

## Scroll-stopping visual principles

Every visual must use as many relevant principles as possible:

- One clear focal point; avoid visual clutter
- Strong foreground/background separation
- Warm, appetizing, realistic light
- Visible texture: food, paper, packaging, tile, steam, hands, menus, countertops, aprons,
  storefronts, ingredient prep, labels, printed materials, screens in real environments
- Human presence whenever appropriate: hands preparing food, owner reviewing a menu,
  chef plating, staff in motion, customer interaction, founder working
- Genuine movement for video: stirring, slicing, plating, printing, packing, opening,
  pouring, serving, scrolling through a website, placing a menu, opening a food truck window
- An unexpected angle, contrast, tension, mistake, transformation, or visual question
- Large readable headline only when it strengthens the visual; no dense text blocks
- High contrast and safe margins for mobile viewing
- Brand consistency through typography, color treatment, and composition—not repetitive
  decorative graphics
- Clear connection between visual, hook, caption, and CTA

Design vertically first:
- Reels / TikTok / Stories: 1080 x 1920, 9:16
- Instagram feed carousel: 1080 x 1350, 4:5
- LinkedIn: optimize separately; do not blindly reuse cropped Instagram creative

Preview all concepts as if viewed on a phone screen.

## Approved visual territories

Use these visual territories repeatedly and intentionally:

### 1. Real food-business friction
Show a recognizable pain point:
- A cluttered menu on a counter
- A hard-to-read website viewed on a phone
- A handwritten prep or launch checklist
- An owner staring at a confusing order flow
- Packaging that disappears on a shelf
- Too many competing signs, labels, colors, or menu choices
- A restaurant storefront with unclear information

Do not shame a real business. Use staged, generic, permissioned, or conceptual examples.

### 2. Sensory food craft
Show:
- Steam, texture, knife work, plating, pouring, garnishing, mixing, packing
- Fresh ingredients
- Hands and tools
- Natural kitchen or counter light
- Close-ups that feel tactile and appetizing

The food should support a business insight, not become random food porn.

### 3. Founder/operator reality
Show:
- Menu review
- Website planning
- Packaging decisions
- Food prep before service
- A launch checklist
- A brand board beside ingredients or packaging
- Laptop/phone work in a café, food truck, kitchen, market, or prep space
- Real decision-making moments

### 4. Clear transformation
Use visual contrast:
- Before/after information hierarchy
- Confusing vs. clear menu design
- Generic site screen vs. focused conversion path
- Unclear offer vs. one clear decision
- Scattered content planning vs. organized content system

Never manipulate visuals or present conceptual work as a real client outcome.

### 5. Editorial proof of thought
Show:
- A close-up of a strategy document, moodboard, website wireframe, annotated menu,
  packaging test, content plan, or checklist
- Only use real project material with permission
- Otherwise create an internal demonstration clearly marked as a conceptual example

## Visual formats

### Reels / short-form video

Default style:
- 8–30 seconds
- A visually compelling first frame in the first second
- One core idea
- Natural movement or fast visual progression
- Strong on-screen hook
- Captions/subtitles
- Human voiceover or natural sound when appropriate
- Minimal transitions; do not use distracting template effects
- End with a concise, relevant CTA

Recommended structure:
0–2 seconds: Visual hook + short on-screen problem
2–8 seconds: Show the real-world friction, mistake, or contrast
8–20 seconds: Deliver one practical insight or fix
Final seconds: CTA tied to the exact problem and relevant Sofrito Studio service

### Carousel posts

Default style:
- Slide 1: Bold realistic image plus a short, specific hook
- Slides 2–6: One idea per slide; show real photos, believable mockups, simple comparisons,
  closeups, or process imagery
- Final slide: Clear summary and CTA
- Do not use more text than a mobile viewer can scan quickly
- Avoid repeating the same layout or generic background on every slide

### Static posts

Use when:
- The image itself tells the story
- There is one sharp opinion or insight
- You have a strong real photo, screen recording still, visual comparison, or permissioned proof

### Stories

Use:
- Behind the scenes
- Practical micro-tips
- Polls and questions
- Process moments
- Previews of long-form content
- Permissioned client/project moments
- Directing viewers to an approved CTA or service page

When text appears on an image:
- Use one short message that can be read in under one second
- Use 3–9 words whenever possible
- Make it specific to a food-business decision or problem

- Place text away from platform interface zones

- Do not repeat the entire caption

- "Make the next step obvious."

Avoid:
- "Elevate your brand"
- "Transform your business"
- "Let's grow together"
- "We help brands thrive"
- "Branding matters"
- "New post"
- "Save this!"

## Mandatory Prompt Format

Write every image prompt in this order, using clear labeled sections:

### 1. Intended Use
State:
- Platform
- Aspect ratio
- Post format
- Audience
- Core post message
- Desired viewer action

### 2. Scene / Environment
Describe where the image takes place.
Use a plausible food-business location such as:
- Restaurant prep kitchen
- Café counter
- Food truck service window
- Catering prep table
- Specialty-food packaging table
- Farmers-market stall
- Small restaurant office
- Studio desk beside food packaging and menus
- Front-of-house service area

### 3. Primary Subject and Action
Describe:
- One dominant subject
- What they are doing
- Their relationship to the business insight
- Human details only when needed: age range, clothing, pose, expression, gaze
- If hands are visible, ensure hands have a simple, natural task

Use real-world actions:
- Reviewing a menu
- Plating food
- Labeling packaged goods
- Updating a website on a phone
- Reviewing a brand board
- Preparing for service
- Writing a launch checklist
- Photographing a dish
- Packing customer orders
- Comparing two menu/website options

### 4. Visible Details and Materials
Specify only meaningful details:
- Food and ingredients
- Menu paper and typography treatment, without readable copy unless requested
- Packaging materials
- Aprons, tile, wood, stainless steel, ceramic, produce crates
- Laptop, phone, receipt printer, order ticket, labels, tape, pen, notebook
- Texture, wear, steam, crumbs, fingerprints, paper grain, fabric, realistic kitchen mess

Use physical details to make the scene credible and tactile.

### 5. Composition and Camera
Specify:
- Orientation and exact aspect ratio
- Framing: close-up, medium shot, wide, top-down, over-the-shoulder, macro
- Viewpoint: eye level, low angle, tabletop angle, behind-the-counter angle
- Dominant focal point
- Foreground, midground, and background
- Negative space for headline, if applicable
- Depth of field
- Lens feel, as a visual cue only: 35mm documentary, 50mm editorial, macro food detail

Default social formats:
- Reels, Stories, TikTok: vertical 9:16, 1080 x 1920
- Instagram carousel/feed: portrait 4:5, 1080 x 1350
- LinkedIn: use a separately composed 1.91:1, 1200 x 627, or square 1:1 version

### 6. Lighting and Mood
Specify:
- Source: window light, warm practical kitchen light, early-morning café light, late-afternoon sun, evening service lighting
- Direction: side-lit, backlit, soft overhead, window light from camera left
- Quality: soft diffuse, natural contrast, subtle shadows, slightly imperfect light
- Mood: focused, calm, tactile, busy, hopeful, candid, practical, warm

Avoid sterile studio lighting unless creating a product shot.

### 7. Brand Treatment
Specify:
- Sofrito Studio palette influence: warm neutrals, earthy red, deep green, natural wood, ceramic, paper, stainless steel, tile
- Editorial, hospitality, documentary sensibility
- Visual simplicity and one unmistakable focal point
- No visible logo unless the founder provides an approved logo and explicitly requests it

### 8. Headline / Text Overlay
Use only if it improves the post.
Specify:
- Exact headline in quotation marks
- Maximum 3–9 words
- Placement: upper third, left side, lower third, etc.
- Large editorial sans-serif or approved brand type
- High-contrast and phone-readable
- Safe margins; do not cover focal subject

If no text should appear, state:
"No visible text, lettering, labels, logo, signage, watermark, or typography."

Do not request ambiguous text. Image generators can make spelling mistakes.
For important text, generate the image without words and add final text in a design tool.

### 9. Realism and Quality Constraints
End every prompt with:
"Photorealistic editorial food-and-hospitality photography, captured as a candid real-world moment. Natural skin texture, accurate hands, believable proportions, real food texture, authentic material wear, shallow depth of field where appropriate, high-quality mobile social creative, visually clear at small screen size."

Then include relevant exclusions:
"No line art, no vector graphics, no illustration, no cartoon, no CGI, no 3D render, no plastic skin, no distorted hands, no extra fingers, no warped utensils, no floating objects, no unreadable food, no oversaturated colors, no watermark, no unintended text, no trademarks, no copied packaging, no recognizable client identity."

### 10. Ethical / Asset Status
At the end of every visual brief, state one:
- Original asset required
- Licensed stock asset required
- Conceptual AI image allowed
- Client asset requires documented permission
- Do not generate; use an authentic photo/video instead

Never use AI-generated people, venues, or project work to imply a real Sofrito Studio client, customer, testimonial, result, restaurant, or completed engagement.

# ON-IMAGE TEXT & SPELLING PROTOCOL

## Non-negotiable rule

Do not trust an image model as the final authority for exact spelling.

The agent must choose one of two production paths before generating an image:

### Path A — Text-free image + deterministic overlay
Use by default for:
- URLs, including sofritostudio.com
- Calls to action
- Brand names
- Service names
- Prices
- Dates and times
- Names of people, places, products, or clients
- Statistics
- Legal or regulatory statements
- Testimonials
- More than 5 words of visible copy
- Small text
- Multi-slide carousels
- Any text where a single incorrect character creates risk

Generate the visual with no readable text, logos, labels, signs, watermarks, or lettering.
Then add approved copy using a deterministic typography layer in HTML/CSS, SVG, Canvas, Figma, Canva, or the project's approved design workflow.

This is the default for Sofrito Studio social content.

### Path B — Image-generated text
Use only when:
- The text is a short visual hook of 1–5 common words
- The text is not legally, financially, or commercially critical
- Exact spelling is not essential to the meaning
- The founder approves image-model text generation for that asset
- The output can be inspected at final social-post size

Examples:
- "Make it clear."
- "Your menu matters."
- "Before you launch."
- "Make the next step obvious."

Do not use Path B for:
- "sofritostudio.com"
- Prices
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- QR codes
- Dates
- Promotional codes
- Client names
- Testimonials
- Fine print
- Hashtags
- Long headlines
- Dense carousel copy

## Spelling verification protocol

Before any asset is marked ready for approval:

1. Extract every visible string from the rendered image using OCR or visual inspection.
2. Compare extracted text character-for-character with `exact_copy`.
3. Confirm:
   - Every required string appears exactly once
   - No required character is missing
   - No extra characters appear
   - No unintended words/numbers/logos appear
   - Capitalization and punctuation are correct
   - Line breaks are intentional
   - Text is readable on a mobile-size preview
   - Text has adequate contrast
   - Text stays within platform-safe areas

4. Record the result in the asset record.

### Pass/fail rule

PASS only if:
- Every business-critical string matches exactly
- The copy is legible at final intended size
- There is no unwanted generated text
- Contrast and safe margins pass
- The visual and text match the approved post brief

FAIL if:
- One character is wrong
- The website URL is wrong or unclear
- Any visible price/date/name is wrong
- Text is cut off, unreadable, too small, or too low contrast
- The model adds invented labels, logos, words, signs, or badges

A failed visual must not be scheduled or published.

## Slide architecture

Every carousel should follow a deliberate narrative, not a pile of tips.

Default 7-slide structure:

1. Hook
   - One specific, high-recognition problem or contrarian insight
   - Large headline
   - Strong visual focal point
   - Optional "Swipe →" cue
   - No dense explanation

2. Problem
   - Name the cost, friction, or mistake
   - Make the target food-business owner feel recognized

3. Reframe
   - Explain why the obvious fix is incomplete or wrong

4. Practical principle
   - Give one useful rule, framework, or decision filter

5. Example
   - Show a clear, ethical, conceptual, or permissioned illustration of the principle

6. Action
   - Give a short checklist or an immediate next step

7. CTA
   - Recap the useful insight
   - Direct the qualified viewer to the relevant Sofrito Studio service, guide, or website page
   - Use a clear but not pushy CTA

Do not force seven slides. Use 4–8 slides unless a different count has a clear reason.

Each slide must:
- Make sense if viewed alone
- Advance the narrative if viewed in sequence
- Contain one dominant idea
- Use limited, scannable text
- Have a clear reading order
- Retain visual consistency without becoming repetitive

---

## Layout rules

For every slide:
- Use the same safe margin and grid.
- Establish a clear reading order: eyebrow → headline → body → CTA/slide marker.
- Align elements consistently to the grid.
- Use intentional whitespace.
- Keep the headline within the upper or middle visual priority zone.
- Place body copy in a visually quiet area.
- Never place copy over busy food detail, faces, hands, menus, packaging, or product details.
- Use a gradient, panel, blur, crop shift, or image darkening only when needed for contrast.
- Do not use drop shadows, outlines, or multiple effects as a substitute for contrast.
- Do not let lines break awkwardly.
- Avoid leaving one short word alone on a final line when a better line break is possible.
- Keep text and CTA inside safe areas.
- Use slide numbers sparingly and consistently: `01/07`, not a mix of styles.
- Keep the CTA meaningful; never use a final slide that says only "Follow for more."

---

## Visual-to-type relationship

Select the layout based on the visual:

### Text beside subject
Use when the image has clear negative space.
- Place copy opposite the focal subject.
- Keep text blocks narrow enough for fast reading.
- Do not cover hands, food, faces, menus, packaging, or product details.

### Text on image
Use only with sufficient quiet space.
- Add a restrained solid or gradient overlay.
- Verify contrast at mobile size.
- Use short copy only.

### Text on panel
Use for educational or body-copy-heavy slides.
- Use warm paper/cream panels with dark ink copy.
- Keep photography visible as supporting context.
- Do not make every slide a plain text card.

### Full-bleed visual
Use for the hook, example, or visual-reset slide.
- Use only a short headline or no text.
- Maintain one focal subject.
- Keep visual relevance to the food-business insight.

## Image-generation interface

When generating carousel background imagery:
1. Create the background image without any readable wording.
2. Explicitly reserve empty visual space for overlay text.
3. State where the text will be added later.
4. Provide the slide number, role, and layout direction.
5. Use photo-realistic editorial food-and-hospitality direction.
6. Do not request type, signs, menus, labels, logos, watermark, or letters.

Required instruction in every visual-generation prompt:

"No visible words, letters, menu text, label text, signage, logo, watermark, URL, price, or typography. Reserve clear negative space in the [exact location] for a deterministic text overlay added later."

## Copy-length controls

Before layout, validate each slide:

### Hook slide
- Eyebrow: 0–5 words
- Headline: 3–12 words
- Supporting text: 0–18 words

### Educational slide
- Headline: 3–10 words
- Body: 15–40 words
- One idea only

### Checklist/action slide
- Headline: 3–8 words
- Maximum 3–5 short points
- Each point: 2–8 words

### CTA slide
- Headline: 3–10 words
- Supporting line: 0–20 words
- CTA: 2–8 words

If copy exceeds the limit:
1. Cut unnecessary words.
2. Split the idea into another slide.
3. Replace explanation with a visual example.
4. Do not shrink type to make it fit.

Reject and revise any slide that fails a check.
